## Relevant Industry Standards or Protocols

- **4R Nutrient Stewardship** — right source, right rate, right time, right place framework for fertiliser management advisory
- **IPM (Integrated Pest Management)** — science-based decision framework for crop protection recommendations underlying advisory platforms
- **NDVI and Remote Sensing Standards** — vegetation indices (NDVI, NDRE, SAVI) used in satellite-based crop health monitoring
- **FAO Global Soil Partnership (GSP)** — international framework and data standards for soil health monitoring
- **ISO 11074 / ISO 10381** — soil quality characterisation and sampling standards referenced in soil health assessment
- **IPPC (International Plant Protection Convention)** — global standards for pest reporting and phytosanitary measures informing disease advisory systems
- **Open Geospatial Consortium (OGC) Standards** — spatial data interoperability standards for field boundary and GIS data exchange

## Market Research

**Market Size:** The global precision agriculture market (the primary container for agronomy advisory software) was valued at approximately USD 12 billion in 2025 and is projected to exceed USD 25 billion by 2030 at a CAGR of around 13%. AI-specific agronomy tools represent a fast-growing subsegment.

**Funding:** Climate FieldView (Bayer) and Granular (Corteva) are backed by major agrochemical corporations. Cropin raised over $25 million; Farmonaut operates on a lean model with government partnership funding. Significant investment flows through agri-tech accelerators and government digital agriculture initiatives (EU Farm to Fork, USDA digital ag programmes).

**Pricing Landscape:** Consumer-grade tools (Farmonaut) offer subscription tiers from a few dollars per month. Mid-market platforms (Climate FieldView) charge per-acre fees ($5–$15/acre/yr). Enterprise and precision ag tools (CropX, Trimble, Granular) are custom-quoted, typically $10,000–$100,000+/yr depending on farm scale.

**Key Buyer Personas:** Commercial crop advisors and agronomists serving farmer clients; large-scale row crop, horticulture, and specialty crop growers; cooperative and input retailer agronomy teams; crop insurance underwriters needing yield and risk data; food company procurement teams requiring sustainability and quality data from suppliers.

**Notable Trends:** In 2026, agronomy platforms are converging on real-time decision support integrating satellite, drone, sensor, and weather data. AI-powered disease and pest identification from smartphone images is mainstream. Yield optimisation models now incorporate economic as well as agronomic variables. Sustainability and carbon reporting from agronomic data is an emerging requirement from food company buyers.

## AI-Native Opportunity

- Disease and pest identification from smartphone-captured field images using computer vision models trained on large agronomic image datasets, providing instant identification and treatment recommendations without an agronomist visit
- Yield prediction models combining historical yield maps, soil variability layers, weather forecasts, and current crop health indices to generate field-by-field harvest estimates weeks in advance
- LLM-powered agronomic advisory chatbot that translates complex soil test results and crop scouting observations into plain-language, actionable recommendations for non-expert farmers
- Adaptive variable-rate prescription generation that learns from grower acceptance or rejection of recommendations over time, personalising advice to local agronomic conditions and management practices
- Soil carbon sequestration quantification combining soil sampling, satellite indices, and agronomic practice records to generate carbon credit documentation for voluntary markets
